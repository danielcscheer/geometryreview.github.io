Unit Conversion

  • Convert measurements within the metric system
The metric staircase

Every metric unit sits on this "staircase." Moving right = smaller units (multiply). Moving left = bigger units (divide). Each step is a factor of 10.

Kilo×1000
Hecto×100
Deka×10
BASE(m, g, L)
Deci×0.1
Centi×0.01
Milli×0.001
KingHenryDiedByDrinkingChocolateMilk

Kilo–Hecto–Deka–base–Deci–Centi–Milli. Count the steps between your starting unit and target unit, then move the decimal that many places (right = bigger number, left = smaller number).

Example
Convert 2.3 kilograms to grams.
1
Grams is the base unit. Kilo is 3 steps to the left of base.
2
Going from kilo → base is moving right 3 steps, so move the decimal point 3 places right.
3
Fill empty spots with zeros: 2.3 → 23 → 230 → 2300.
2.3 kg = 2300 g

Area & Perimeter

  • Perimeter = distance around a shape. Area = space inside a shape (always in square units).
  • Base and height are always perpendicular (they form a right angle).
Formulas
Triangle — Area
A = 12bh
Rectangle / Square / Parallelogram — Area
A = bh
Circle — Area
A = πr²
Circle — Circumference
C = πd or 2πr

b = base, h = height, r = radius, d = diameter. If your calculator has no π key, use π ≈ 3.14.

Example 1
A parallelogram has two sides of 6 in, two sides of 10 in, and a height of 5 in (perpendicular to the 10 in side).
1
Perimeter: add all four sides.
P = 6 + 6 + 10 + 10 = 32 in
2
Area: identify base and height — b = 10 in, h = 5 in.
A = bh = 10 × 5 = 50 in²
Example 2
A triangle has an area of 44.5 cm² and a height of 7.8 cm. Find the base.
1
Plug into the triangle area formula.
44.5 = 12(b)(7.8)
2
Multiply both sides by 2, then divide by 7.8.
89 ÷ 7.8 = b
b ≈ 11.4 cm
Practice — find area and perimeter (or circumference)

  • Find volume of a prism, cylinder, sphere, and cone
Formulas
General Prism
V = Bh (B = area of the base)
Cylinder
V = πr²h
Sphere
V = 43πr³
Cone
V = 13πr²h
Example 1
A rectangular prism has length 12 cm, width 4 cm, height 5 cm.
1
Volume of a prism = (area of base) × height = (length × width) × height.
2
Plug in and solve.
V = (12 × 4) × 5 = 240 cm³

1

Slope

  • Find slope given two coordinate points
  • Find slope given a graph of two points
Formula (memorize this — it's not on the Regents reference sheet!)

Slope = riserun = y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁

Given two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), subtract the y's on top and the x's on the bottom — just keep the order consistent.

Example 1
Find the slope of the line through (6, 2) and (−4, 8).
1
Plug into the slope formula.
m = (8 − 2) / (−4 − 6) = 6 / −10
2
Reduce the fraction.
m = −3/5
Example 2 — reading a graph
Find the slope of the line below.
1
Start at one point. Count boxes up/down to the next point for rise (up = positive, down = negative).
2
Count boxes left/right for run (right = positive, left = negative).
3
From (−3,−2) to (3,4): rise = 6, run = 6.
m = 6/6 = 1
Practice — find the slope. Simplify all answers.

m = 2
2

Show solution

m = −2
3

Show solution
m = (3−(−5))/(4−0) = 8/4

Equation of a Line

  • Write the equation of a line in slope-intercept form
  • Write the equation of a line in point-slope form
Slope-Intercept Form

y = mx + b

m = slope, b = y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis).

Example 1
Write the equation of the line with slope −2/3 and y-intercept −6.
1
Plug slope into m and y-intercept into b.
y = −(2/3)x − 6
Example 2
Write the equation of the line with slope 1/2 that passes through (2, 3).
1
Plug the slope into m, and the point into x and y, then solve for b.
3 = (1/2)(2) + b → 3 = 1 + b → b = 2
2
Plug m and b back into slope-intercept form.
y = (1/2)x + 2
Example 3 — rearranging into slope-intercept form
Write −2y + 8 = 7x in slope-intercept form.
1
Subtract 8 from both sides.
−2y = 7x − 8
2
Divide everything by −2.
y = −(7/2)x + 4
Point-Slope Form

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Comes straight from the slope formula. Handy when you're given a slope and one point, or two points and no y-intercept.

Example 4
Find the equation of the line with slope −3 through the point (4, −10).
1
Plug the slope into m and the point into x₁, y₁.
y − (−10) = −3(x − 4) → y + 10 = −3(x − 4)
Example 5
Find the equation of the line through (0, 1) and (3, −3).
1
First find the slope.
m = (−3 − 1)/(3 − 0) = −4/3
2
Pick either point and plug into point-slope form.
y − 1 = −(4/3)(x − 0)

Radicals

  • Simplify radicals
  • Multiply radicals
  • Add and subtract radicals
Perfect squares to know cold:

1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, ...

Steps to simplify a radical
  1. Is the number under the root a perfect square? If yes — take the root, done.
  2. If not, is it divisible by a perfect square? Pull out the largest one you can find and simplify that piece.
  3. If it's not divisible by any perfect square, it's already in simplest form.
Example 1
Simplify √80
1
80 isn't a perfect square, but it's divisible by the perfect square 16.
√80 = √(16 · 5)
2
Take the root of the perfect square piece; leave the rest under the radical.
= 4√5
Steps to multiply radicals
  1. Multiply the coefficients (numbers outside the root). No visible coefficient means it's 1.
  2. Multiply the radicands (numbers inside the root).
  3. Simplify the result.
Example 2
Multiply 2√8 · 5√6
1
Multiply coefficients: 2 × 5 = 10.
2
Multiply radicands: 8 × 6 = 48.
10√48
3
Simplify √48 = √(16·3) = 4√3, then multiply by the coefficient.
10 · 4√3 = 40√3
Steps to add / subtract radicals
  1. If the radicands match, add/subtract the coefficients (like combining like terms).
  2. If they don't match, simplify each radical first — they might match after simplifying.
Example 3
Simplify 3√20 + 6√5 − √15
1
Simplify √20 = √(4·5) = 2√5, so 3√20 = 6√5.
6√5 + 6√5 − √15
2
Combine the matching radicands (√5 and √5). √15 stays separate — it doesn't match.
= 12√5 − √15

Pythagorean Theorem

  • Use the Pythagorean theorem to find a missing side of a right triangle
Formula

a² + b² = c²

a and b are the legs of the triangle; c is always the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle — the longest side).

Example
A right triangle has legs x and 12 yd, and hypotenuse 15 yd. Find x.
1
Set up the equation.
x² + 12² = 15²
2
Simplify the squares.
x² + 144 = 225
3
Subtract 144 from both sides.
x² = 81
4
Take the square root of both sides.
x = 9
Practice — find the missing side
1

Show solution
c² = 6²+8² = 36+64 = 100
c = 10
2
leg 9, hypotenuse 15 — find the other leg
Show solution
b² = 15²−9² = 225−81 = 144
b = 12
3
legs 5 and 12 — find the hypotenuse
Show solution
c² = 25+144 = 169
c = 13
4
leg 8, hypotenuse 17 — find the other leg
Show solution
b² = 289−64 = 225
b = 15
5
legs 20 and 21 — find the hypotenuse
Show solution
c² = 400+441 = 841
c = 29
6 · Word problem
A 13 ft ladder leans against a wall, with its base 5 ft from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?
Show solution
height² = 13²−5² = 169−25 = 144

  • Factor a difference of perfect squares

Method 1 — Greatest Common Factor
Factor 12x² + 18x
1
Find the largest number/variable that divides every term: GCF = 6x.
2
Pull it out front, divide each term by it.
6x(2x + 3)
Method 2 — Difference of Squares
Factor x² − 49
1
Recognize the pattern: a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b). Here a = x, b = 7.
2
Write the two factors.
(x − 7)(x + 7)

Factor x² + 7x + 12
1
Find two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7 → 3 and 4.
2
Write as two binomials.
(x + 3)(x + 4)

Solving Quadratic Equations

  • Solve by factoring
  • Solve by using the quadratic formula
  • Solve by completing the square
As with Factoring, this section's packet examples are in "2 of 2" — the walkthroughs below teach the same three methods listed in the packet.
Method 1 — Factoring
Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0
1
Factor the trinomial (numbers that multiply to 6, add to 5 → 2 and 3).
(x + 2)(x + 3) = 0
2
Zero Product Property: if two things multiply to 0, one of them must be 0.
x + 2 = 0 or x + 3 = 0
x = −2 or x = −3
Method 2 — The Quadratic Formula

x = −b ± √(b² − 4ac)2a

Works on any quadratic ax² + bx + c = 0, even ones that don't factor nicely.

Example
Solve 2x² + 3x − 2 = 0
1
Identify a, b, c.
a = 2, b = 3, c = −2
2
Find the discriminant b² − 4ac.
3² − 4(2)(−2) = 9 + 16 = 25
3
Plug into the formula.
x = (−3 ± √25) / 4 = (−3 ± 5) / 4
x = 1/2 or x = −2
Method 3 — Completing the Square
Solve x² + 6x + 5 = 0
1
Move the constant to the other side.
x² + 6x = −5
2
Take half of the x-coefficient, square it, add to both sides. (6/2)² = 9.
x² + 6x + 9 = 4
3
The left side is now a perfect square trinomial.
(x + 3)² = 4
4
Square root both sides — don't forget ±.
x + 3 = ±2
x = −1 or x = −5
Practice

x = 3 or x = −2
2 · Factoring

x = −4

Show solution

x = 1 or x = −1/3
